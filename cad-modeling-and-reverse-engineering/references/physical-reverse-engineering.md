# Reverse-engineering parts from a physical sample

Dated sessions are provenance. Re-verify against the actual part, files, slicer and material.

## 1. Replacement parts: identify first, measure humanely

- Identify the exact OEM part number before treating a marketplace model as compatible. Manuals and service catalogues often confirm a part number and compatibility but omit dimensions; an exploded view or product photo is not a dimensioned drawing.
- For water-adjacent parts, define the function and sealing boundary first. A small drain plug, a pressure-side cap, a prefilter lid and a tank cover are not interchangeable because they share a product family. Search the model number with generic part terms and official spare-part codes, confirm against the parts list and service catalogue, and look for a ready-made model that matches that code. If none is verified, do a physical reconstruction instead of recommending a visually similar cap.
- Make measuring easy and error-tolerant. Do not hand over an unannotated list of ambiguous diameters, especially when the mating device is unavailable. First request orthographic top, underside and side photos with a ruler or caliper as scale plus one close-up of a locking lug or thread. Then provide an annotated sketch or parametric preview showing exactly which surfaces need dimensions. Define whether height includes a handle separately from the sealing geometry. A successful fit coupon is more reliable than a nominal measurement of a flexible or complex part. For a critical fit, print a small fit coupon or ring before the full-size part and validate it through the existing fit owner (`fit-clearance-and-retention.md` and `design-and-fit-gates.md`), not through a separate procedure here. Ask short numbered lists, name only the most important measurements, mark optional ones separately, and for a third party say that photos with calipers and approximate values are useful.
- For a cover that seals on an original O-ring, keep and reuse the original gasket if sound; model the groove and land only after verifying fit. Use a material suitable for water, UV and temperature (for example PETG, ASA or ABS rather than PLA) for outdoor water-system parts. A printed replacement first gets only a dry, low-risk fit check, and that check is depressurised. A good fit, a suitable material and a leak or air-ingress observation are not permission for live pressure-side use: an unrated prototype gets no operating-pressure test and no pressure-side use at all. Pressure-side use needs a design, material and application appropriate and rated for that duty, and a competent, safe test procedure, settled first (this reference does not supply one). A suction-side prefilter leak can cause loss of prime without visible spray; treat that as a reason to review the part, not to test it live.

## 2. Threaded caps with only the cap available

Make the **first** artifact a male threaded mandrel that screws into the original cap, not a replacement cap.

- Keep axial references independent: open rim to centre of the first thread turn (lead-in position), pitch (centre to centre on the same helix), start count (separate entries at the rim), usable turns or end of thread zone, and the O-ring groove position and size. An O-ring next to the thread is not evidence of where the thread starts or ends. Mark uncertain diameters explicitly; a root-diameter estimate implying implausibly deep threads should not be copied into v1.
- First coupon: measured pitch, start count and first-turn position; one continuous helix (not several overlapping one-turn helices); slightly undersize major diameter so the original cap is not damaged or jammed; a broad shallow trapezoidal or rounded profile unless a profile imprint says otherwise (not a sharp metric V by default); a smaller handle or base that cannot collide with the cap interior; no O-ring land or pressure boundary in the first thread-only coupon.
- Validate the export: finite bounds, zero boundary edges, zero non-manifold edges. A watertight mesh can still slice into near-zero-thickness ribbons or disconnected-looking helical strips, so require the user's slicer layer preview to show a visibly solid continuous helix. A generated section render is secondary evidence; verify it uses the intended plane and do not infer support-freedom from `watertight`, bounds or a mislabelled section plot.
- Parameter names state the physical surface (`tube_od`, `bore_id`, `thread_crest_od`, `pitch`, `axial_thread_width`), not ambiguous `major` and `root`. For a swept helix verify the declared crest equals the actual outer profile edge; centring the profile on the tube radius can silently give a much smaller crest. Check both exported bounds and slicer preview.
- **Internal-collar and single-wall gate.** Do not invent a mating-side collar because the cap contains one. A common arrangement has the O-ring on an internal cap collar while the pump presents a single threaded tube that slides *between* that collar and the cap's female thread. Keep independent: cap internal-collar OD → coupon bore ID; coupon tube OD → must pass under the cap's inner-thread constriction; thread crest OD → must reach the female groove. A coupon can fail before its threads touch (tube OD too large, or bore collides with the collar). Diagnose that as radial clearance, not pitch or profile, and change one radial variable at a time.
- **Interpret feedback by contact order.** If bore clearance feels right but the tube reaches the O-ring or end before the threads carry engagement, do not first add turns or alter the good bore. Separate (a) insufficient radial thread projection (the cap can pull off) from (b) a functional, protruding thread start that is axially too far from the rim. Once radial retention is confirmed, move the whole helix toward the rim in one small axial-only step. Do not change tube, bore, pitch, depth and start together. A shallow hand-tight stop does not prove sealing: retention, axial bottoming and O-ring compression are separate observations.
- **Functional-start verification.** Do not infer the usable thread start from a CAD profile's centre or root edge: a swept trapezoid can begin at the tube surface while its first load-bearing protruding crest is millimetres farther from the rim. Inspect the slicer measurement or section from the rim to that edge. If slicer and arithmetic disagree, treat the slicer as a finding: stop issuing coupons, reconcile the sweep-plane orientation, then make one axial change. Say which edge is measured (root, crest, first load-bearing flank).
- **Thread coupon iteration.** If a full mandrel fails to engage, isolate the profile with a one-turn coupon before touching the O-ring land. Change one dimension per coupon (crest diameter, root depth, axial width, lead-in). When a real solid thread is "too wide", first reduce axial profile width, preserve pitch unless engagement evidence says otherwise.
- Record feedback operationally: catches first turn (yes/no); number of turns engaged; radial feel (tight, correct, loose); failure mode (uniform resistance, one-sided bind, skips thread). Adjust only the implicated variable: major diameter for uniform tight or loose, start or handedness for failure to engage, pitch for progressive binding, profile depth or flank width after diameter and pitch are credible.
- PLA is acceptable for a rapid thread-fit-only coupon, printed upright with several walls and no supports. It is not a water-system part and is never tested on a live pump. A PLA success validates mating geometry only; any water-system replacement needs separate material, sealing and leak testing.

## 3. Small compound spur gears

- Treat **tooth count plus outside diameter** as primary evidence. For a standard external full-depth spur gear, `module = OD / (z + 2)`; compute it from every intact gear. If they agree, keep the fitted module (a fitted 0.492 mm predicts the measured ODs; do not round to 0.5 without a mating or centre-distance test). Keep a nominal-module alternative only if a verified mating gear contradicts the samples.
- A 20° pressure angle is a reasonable *provisional* default, not proof; an initial radial dedendum of `1.25 × module` is conventional, while OEM root shape and undercut on tiny pinions may differ.
- Required evidence before declaring a replacement final: tooth count on both wheels (mark a tooth and count once around); OD tip to tip in at least two orientations; top, side and underside photos with scale; axial facts (thickness of each gear, total height, the Z datum of each, whether the pinion spans the total height or starts above the large gear, from a side-on caliper photo); the mating shaft (round, D-shaped, splined, keyed; do not infer a D-flat from glare); duty cycle, load and temperature before choosing material.
- Model parametrically: module, tooth counts, pressure angle, root clearance, each Z thickness and datum, bore shape and diameter, FDM bore compensation. Generate real involute teeth when the part must mesh with an existing gear.
- First physical gate: print the large gear flat so profiles form in XY; for tiny teeth use fine layers (roughly 0.08–0.12 mm on a 0.4 mm nozzle), several walls, solid infill. Use PETG, ASA or nylon where load, heat or wear matter; PLA is a low-load fitting prototype. Test bore fit and hand meshing before powering the mechanism. Change one variable per iteration (bore compensation, module, axial stack).
- Ship editable source plus exported STL, the fitted measurement arithmetic and assumptions, a short test protocol, and an independent export check (one component, watertight, consistent winding, positive volume, bounds matching OD and height).

## 4. Project hygiene for iterative fit work

Keep a self-contained folder: `cad/` parametric sources, `stl/released/` for the current print candidate, `stl/superseded/` for rejected versions, `stl/dev/` for probes, `docs/` for measurements, iterations and test results, `validation/` for readback checks and hashes. Record physical outcomes separately from CAD assumptions.
