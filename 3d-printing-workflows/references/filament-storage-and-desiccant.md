# Filament dry storage, desiccant sizing, monitoring and containers

Optional depth for storage questions. Numbers are reference values or illustrations from specific projects; measure your own materials and enclosures. The geometry of storage inserts (fit coupons, trays, mounts) lives in the geometry skill if installed (`cad-modeling-and-reverse-engineering`). Sensors, thresholds and example box sizes here are illustrative.

## 1. Three different jobs

Separate **drying wet filament** (needs controlled heat and time suitable for the polymer; room-temperature desiccant is not a substitute), **maintaining already-dry filament** (a genuinely sealed enclosure plus regenerated desiccant) and **recovering quickly after opening** (more exposed desiccant area or forced airflow). Passive desiccant is storage maintenance, not drying.

For storage-only single-spool boxes, rollers, bearings, PTFE outlets and feed hardware are unnecessary: a simple cradle or perforated false floor over a broad shallow desiccant tray is enough. Bearings reduce rolling friction when printing from a box; removing them is a simplification for storage, not a friction upgrade.

## 2. Desiccant behaviour

- Water vapour diffuses through a small sealed box, so gel need not surround the spool. Geometry mainly changes response time: a deep tightly packed cup exposes little bead area; a broad perforated tray or ventilated cartridge improves kinetics; forced flow improves kinetics further but does not raise total water capacity or repair leaks.
- Orange indicating gel changes colour gradually and is not a calibrated RH gauge. Some products only show a clear transition after roughly 6–8 % mass water uptake. Track regenerated dry mass when practical; colour and cheap hygrometers are secondary signals. If replacing apparently orange gel lowers RH, the old batch was likely partly loaded.
- Around 20 % RH is already useful storage for common PLA and PETG.
- A forced-air desiccant cartridge on a blower (a low-static-pressure axial fan is poor through packed beads) can drop RH quickly in a large cabinet, but cabinet sealing, dry incoming spools, enough desiccant mass and regeneration cadence still decide long-term results. One public video ([reported example](https://youtu.be/8kIDLIXILKo); not reproduced or verified here, and not a universal result) showed a passive tray bringing a large cabinet from 58 % to about 38–40 % overnight while one blower cartridge took it from 42 % to 27 % in 15 minutes; RH later rose when spools and cardboard added moisture. Treat such results as evidence of fast recovery, not greater equilibrium capacity. Prefer passive storage first for individual small boxes; forced air becomes attractive for one multi-spool cabinet (removable regenerated cartridges, and a separate heated dryer for wet filament). Molecular sieve can be evaluated for low-RH polishing; silica gel remains economical bulk capture.
- Public provenance for the orange-gel figures (background, not a batch certificate or a derived isotherm): Canadian Conservation Institute, [*Silica Gel: Passive Control of Relative Humidity*, Technical Bulletin 33, Table 3](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/conservation-preservation-publications/technical-bulletins/silica-gel-relative-humidity.html) for orange-gel equilibrium moisture at 20 °C; and the supplier's [Scharlau/EGT orange indicator gel TDS](https://shop.egtchemie.ch/getfile/GE0042_TDS_EN.pdf) (version 2015.3.9: minimum 27 % water absorption in 24 h at 80 % RH, bulk density ~800 kg/m³). The TDS does not support a minimum absorption claim at 40 % RH.

## 3. Sizing the desiccant

Mass and exposure answer different questions. **Recovery kinetics** (time to reduce RH) depends on exposed area, lid and mesh resistance, bed depth and boundary layers. **Service interval** (time until regeneration) depends on usable capacity, vapour ingress, openings and moisture released by the spool and its cardboard. Do not copy a fixed mass from one prototype.

- Enclosure air is a small load: at about 25 °C saturated air holds roughly 23 g H2O/m³, so `water = 23 × V(m³) × (RH_initial − RH_target)` with V in m³ and the relative humidities as dimensionless fractions between 0 and 1 (not percentages); for 4 L (0.004 m³) and 40 % to 10 % (0.40 to 0.10) that is 23 × 0.004 × (0.40 − 0.10) = 0.0276 g, about 0.03 g. Long-term capacity goes to vapour transmission, seal leakage, openings, spool moisture and incomplete regeneration.
- Capacity from an isotherm: use the exact product's if available. One reference table for orange silica gel at 20 °C (conservation-science technical bulletin) gave equilibrium moisture of about 4.45 % at 10 % RH, 8.94 % at 20 %, 13.86 % at 30 %, 19.10 % at 40 % and 24.14 % at 50 % RH (g water per 100 g dry gel). Usable capacity is `m_gel × EMC(threshold)` minus any initial moisture. Treat reference values as sizing priors, not certificates for a consumer batch, and replace them with measurements.
- Service interval: `required gel mass = safety_factor × (initial_water_load + daily_ingress × target_days) / usable_capacity_fraction`. For illustration, with a dry-start 20 % threshold and 0.10 g/day ingress the idealised interval is on the order of 45, 80, 107 and 134 days for 50, 90, 120 and 150 g; do not present this as a prediction until ingress is measured.
- Bed depth: `depth = gel_mass / (bulk_density × bed_area)`; orange gel bulk density is about 0.8 g/cm³ (measure yours). Optimise a broad shallow layer first, then compute mass from the measured moisture load. A broad roughly 10–15 mm bed is a design constraint; a deeper mound slows access to lower beads. Keep cells shallow and connected to the common headspace with distributed perforation, not sealed pockets.
- Measure the ingress rate with an empty-box test rather than trusting a marketing claim: regenerate and cool a known mass in sealed storage, weigh it, load a representative shallow bed in the closed candidate box without filament, log ambient and internal RH and temperature, keep it closed for a known multi-day interval after the initial transient, recover and weigh promptly (regenerate to stable mass to confirm absorbed water), estimate ingress as water gained divided by days, and repeat or use parallel terminated samples instead of reopening one box. Then run a loaded phase with an equally dried, representative spool; the difference estimates the spool and cardboard burden.
- Choosing mass: for the same container compare several masses holding batch, regeneration, sensor placement and moisture challenge constant; record time to target, stable RH, time to the regeneration threshold, absorbed water per gram and clearance. Choose the smallest mass that meets the target interval with acceptable recovery and clearance.
- Pitfalls: a fast empty-box RH drop proves kinetics, not months of capacity; air-tight claims do not provide vapour ingress; do not add an undried spool mid-test; lower stable RH does not always mean more mass is useful (sensor floor and offset, isotherm).

## 4. Lid perforation (reduced-order comparison)

A sealed room-temperature box without a fan has no defined flow field; vapour diffusion, weak convection, packed-bed transport, adsorption kinetics and leakage dominate. Detailed airflow simulation is false precision without full geometry, leak conductance, porosity and tortuosity, effective diffusivity and isotherm data. As a comparison model use diffusion resistances in series:

```text
R_boundary = L_boundary / (D_air × A_lid)
R_plate    = L_plate    / (D_air × A_open)
R_bed      = L_bed      / (D_bed × A_lid)
tau        = V_air × (R_boundary + R_plate + R_bed)
t(initial to target) = tau × ln[(RH_initial − RH_eq) / (RH_target − RH_eq)]
```

Use a water-vapour diffusivity in air of roughly 2.5e-5 m²/s as an exposed input; boundary thickness, free volume and bed effective diffusivity are uncertain, so report an envelope (one run swept free volume 2–4 L, boundary 10–50 mm and bed factor 0.2–0.5 and got an 8.6–41 minute characteristic-time envelope). Compute porosity from the generated CAD: `A_open = slot_count × slot_length × slot_width`, `porosity = A_open / lid_plan_area`; in the actual open-area check also confirm that geometrical rails and support posts do not materially occlude the lid openings. In the worked example about 10 % porosity gave a time constant near 16 minutes and doubling to 20 % cut it only about 11 %, with packed-bed and boundary resistance dominating above roughly 10 %, so a mechanically conservative lid near 10 % was kept for the first trial. Do not optimise perforation from simulation alone: compare sensors together first to measure offset, use equal regenerated mass and equivalently dried spools in two boxes, log without opening, swap sensors midway, fit the exponential response, and make a denser lid only if fresh gel shows a genuinely slow initial decline. A good decline followed by a gradual rise points to capacity or leakage, not perforation.

Diffusivity background (the roughly 2.5e-5 m²/s above remains an exposed comparison input): [EngineeringToolBox, air diffusion coefficient of gas mixtures](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/air-diffusion-coefficient-gas-mixture-temperature-d_2010.html) and [NIST TN 2279](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=957163) (general gas-diffusion background with its temperature and pressure dependence, recommended standard 298.15 K and 101.325 kPa). They do not independently prove packed-bed kinetics, and no exact water–air value is claimed from them unless verified.

## 5. Monitoring and alerts

- Plastic food containers normally pass Zigbee and Z-Wave radio; a battery humidity sensor can sit inside without a cable hole. Confirm the exact sensor actually exposes humidity and its low-RH range (consumer ±2–5 % specifications make it no laboratory instrument below 20 %; protocol support does not imply humidity measurement).
- Cheap 48 × 29 mm panel hygrometers of the same generic family are an OEM cluster, not a dimensional or electrical standard; bezel, depth, side clips, rear vents and battery door differ by several millimetres. Measure the unit you have. Prefer a fully internal removable cradle read through the transparent wall or lid instead of cutting the sealed container; keep rear sensing vents open, mount high and away from the desiccant, preserve battery access, and use a constant mount with a small swappable adapter ring. A displayed 10 % is commonly a lower clamp, so use same-unit baseline trend and a traffic-light regeneration threshold instead of treating low digits as calibrated. For a branded 46 × 46 × 13 mm unit whose entire rear shell snaps off for its coin cell, retain it in a removable open-backed cassette with light side or corner pads: no permanent adhesive, no clamping across the case seam, no closed sleeve over the rear shell, no covered vent banks, no battery replacement while mounted; add an open rear plenum and a finger notch; validate with a small clearance coupon (a vendor's nominal size is not a tolerance specification); test LCD readability through the actual container material (recycled transparent PP can be hazy) before freezing a through-wall mount. Manufacturer accuracy is often specified only in the 40–80 % region.
- Offset characterisation of new displays: place both units side by side in the intended ambient location for approximately two hours and record the RH and temperature offset; do not disturb an active box A/B test merely to characterise them. Afterwards compare them at low RH (about 10–20 %) beside a known trend sensor, and treat a displayed 10 % as a probable lower clamp, not a certified calibration.
- Sustained alerts, suppressing opening spikes: warn when RH stays above about 25 % for several hours; stronger above about 30 % for a shorter period; detect failure to recover after a known opening; notify to inspect or regenerate rather than declaring saturation from one reading. The alert must distinguish a leak, a newly added wet spool, exhausted desiccant and sensor offset. A low-voltage fan, if used, needs hysteresis and minimum run and off times (an example experimental band: start above 22 % after 15 minutes, stop below 17 %; tune from real sensor behaviour).

## 6. Controlled A/B protocol (passive versus active, custom versus original boxes)

1. **Define the claim.** A *practical full-system comparison* lets each design use the maximum desiccant mass it supports and cannot isolate tray geometry. A *geometry or kinetics comparison* needs equal regenerated mass and tightly controlled boxes, contents, placement and openings. Do not mix the estimands.
2. Stage it: practical maximum-capacity round; after multi-day stability swap only the physical sensors between boxes and record the exact time; a second equal-mass round to isolate geometry; if two container bodies are involved, cross over the inserts in a later round to expose seal-to-seal variation.
3. **Record before interpretation:** exact start and closure times; container model and whether bodies and lids are identical; co-location away from different heat, window, fan or printer exposure; empty-box baseline or loaded round (material, spool construction especially cardboard, starting dryness, comparable load); gel batch, regeneration method and time, cooling, storage container and duration afterwards, whether the batch was mixed before division, weighed as-loaded mass; sensor identities, labels, battery state and height from the desiccant; every opening, move, sensor swap or gel change as an append-only event. Mark unrecorded variables unknown instead of reconstructing them. An empty-box round is the cleanest first test; do not add undried filament mid-round. Gel that cooled in a container of unknown airtightness is not "fully dry at start": a shared well-mixed batch still supports a relative comparison but weakens absolute-capacity claims, and end-of-test weighing followed by regeneration to stable mass estimates water release without reconstructing the start. Consistent sensor placement (for example top-centre of the lids, far from the gel) matters more than finding the wettest point.
4. **Measurements:** one RH and temperature sensor per box with a high-resolution raw history; milestone snapshots at baseline, +15 min, +30 min, +1 h, +3 h, +6 h, +12 h, +24 h and +48 h. A written schedule is not monitoring: if the operator wants it, set up and verify the actual checkpoint mechanism (enabled, intended timestamps and delivery) and record the enabled checkpoint, job and callback IDs beside the private test log, not only a prose schedule (nothing is created unless monitoring is separately requested and authorised), log unavailable or error states instead of omitting a sensor, avoid duplicate rows, re-read what you wrote, keep routine writes quiet and reserve notifications for action gates such as a conditional "swap sensors now" that fires only on stable, complete data. Keep raw room and device labels private and aggregate before publishing.
5. **Analysis:** compare time to target RH, minimum stable RH, recovery after an equal opening, temperature and dry-to-final gel mass gain. Do not claim success from a fast drop alone; require multi-day stability. After a sensor swap distinguish a persistent box effect from a reading that follows the sensor. Different gel masses, seals, wet filament or cardboard load and sensor offset are separate causes; do not collapse them into a geometry claim.

## 7. Choosing a container for a public single-spool box

The output is a **physical-test shortlist**, never a compatibility claim.

- **False positives.** Do not select by nominal litres, exterior dimensions, photos or marketplace similarity. A spool can pass the mouth yet collide higher up because of draft, corner radii, ribs, a grip waist or lid intrusion; a full gasket lid can still warp under stacking load; "100 % airtight" claims screen gross leaks only and say nothing about vapour transmission. A brand, EAN, SKU or ASIN is not proof of a frozen mould (tooling, wall thickness, gaskets or suppliers change without a new identifier; listings pool variants and reviews). Public compatibility needs a physical revision fingerprint.
- **Variant, buy-box and landed-price gate.** Verify the exact variant on the destination-country page, not the landing URL or a search result. Record capacity, manufacturer part number and EAN for the selected variant, current price, seller, fulfiller, delivery country and shipping, whether a headline price is domestic-only, and whether the page silently routed to another size. Capture the actual buy box immediately before telling anyone what to order. Separate geometry candidacy from current purchasability and reopen the listing before recommending an order.
- **Delegated research is evidence collection, not authority.** Reopen primary manufacturer and destination-retailer sources for every finalist; audit any derived pass claim against the original dimension drawing; e-commerce metadata can be mislabelled or reordered; if the drawing confirms only exterior dimensions, downgrade to physical-test-only; mark reviewer corrections prominently and keep the reviewed decision separate from the raw helper report.
- **Shortlist** candidates with a manufacturer part number plus EAN or GTIN, a full removable gasket lid and no pour flap, single-unit purchase with a non-destructive return path, availability in several countries and from more than one seller or batch, simple internal walls and floor with clear stacking lands, and enough space for the spool plus insert, sensor and the target 90–150 g payload. Treat ratings cautiously (aggregated across variants).
- **Spool-envelope gate.** "Fits 1 kg" is not a specification. Either support a conservative public gauge (for example diameter 210 mm × width 80 mm) or publish a smaller hard envelope and a tested-spool list. Use real spools and rigid gauges; reusable spools, warped cardboard flanges, labels and outer rings can be larger than a published figure. Require insertion and removal without flexing the box or rubbing the flange, with explicit clearance rather than a friction fit. Express the gate as a section: a free circle of the spool diameter (preferably 10 mm larger) at the intended bed height plus at least about 95 mm clear floor-to-closed-lid height; a published rim dimension can still fail at the floor because of draft, shoulders, ribs and radii. A pessimistic screening prior: one four-clip food box measured about 47 mm smaller on each internal axis than its lid-footprint catalogue dimensions, so unverified external short axes below roughly 260 mm are unlikely to clear a 210 mm spool; it is a prior, not a conversion formula.
- **Physical verification before CAD.** (1) Identity and batch fingerprint: seller, country, order date, EAN, part number, moulded marks, lot marks, separate body and lid mass, photographs; at least two batches or sellers, and for a pan-regional claim samples from several markets; critical contours within the design tolerance (a practical first gate is ±0.5 mm). (2) Internal geometry: horizontal sections at the floor and several heights (for example 0, 5, 20, 50 mm and just below the lid), corner radii, draft, ribs, feet, stacking lands, gasket intrusion, latch bulges; thin coupons or rigid gauges before a full insert; repeat with the intended tray thickness, sensor and full desiccant payload. (3) Seal: paper-strip pull tests around the gasket; water inversion only as a gross-leak screen; controlled RH comparison against a verified baseline with equal gel mass and moisture load; multi-day logging with controlled opening and recovery and a mid-test sensor swap; repeat after at least 200 latch cycles; pass means non-inferior to the baseline under the same protocol (one low reading is no evidence). (4) Loaded stacking and creep: a three-high stack with realistic load for at least a week, recording lid deflection, rocking, latch access, gasket compression and RH, repeated at a warm ambient if boxes will sit near printers. (5) Reorder gate: reorder from another batch or seller and repeat identity, coupon, gauge and seal checks before public release; keep one golden sample and publish the revision fingerprint; add a new compatibility revision if a later sample differs.
- Decision rule: redesign only after one candidate passes identity, spool-envelope, full-payload geometry, seal, cycle and loaded-stack gates. Until then label every marketplace match a purchase candidate and keep these evidence states distinct: reference design, likely clone, purchase candidate, geometry candidate, currently purchasable listing, physically verified specimen.
