# Snapmaker U1 multicolour tuning (optional vendor-specific reference)

Values come from dated research passes (mid-2026) on the U1 with Snapmaker Orca or OrcaSlicer. Treat every number as a starting point to validate, and re-check vendor pages for prices, specifications and availability. General principles are in `multicolor-printer-architecture.md` and `fdm-material-and-diagnostics.md`.

## 1. The machine in brief

Four independent toolheads (SnapSwap toolchanger): colour or material change is a physical head swap rather than unloading and loading through one nozzle, which greatly cuts purge waste and swap time. Commonly cited at research time: a 270 × 270 × 270 mm build volume, 500 mm/s claimed maximum speed, promotional price around 900. Native automatic capacity is four loaded colours or materials. Reviews and community threads broadly agreed it is real and compelling, with caveats about software maturity, an optional accessory for heat-sensitive or high-temperature filaments, no enclosure, TPU reliability, camera quality and unproven long-term toolhead contact durability. Use Snapmaker Orca (an OrcaSlicer fork) first, since toolchanger profiles, toolchange G-code, wipe, park, temperature handling and remote integration are wired; other slicers need correct toolchange macros and profiles and are an advanced path, so do not promise "any slicer just works". Historical source distinction (label it historical and verify the exact fork and version before relying on it): Snapmaker Orca added and advertised Full Spectrum (Beta) from v2.3.3 onward for richer colours with four physical filaments, while the community fork ratdoux/OrcaSlicer-FullSpectrum explores virtual or mixed colours by alternating layer colours. Do not attribute the community mechanism to the vendor build without current evidence. Neither is equivalent to native high colour counts; native physical capacity stays four. For migrating calibrated filament overlays into that fork see `slicer-forks-builds-and-runtime.md`.

## 2. Prime tower starting values (PLA/PETG, 0.4 mm nozzles)

- `Prime volume` start at 20 mm³, validate 15 mm³; `Minimal purge on wipe tower` (per filament, Multimaterial settings) start at 20 mm³, validate 15 mm³. Check both.
- `Width` 25 mm balanced; 30 mm for tall towers; test 20 mm only after volume is proven. `Brim width` about 3 mm normally, 5 mm for tall towers, PETG or marginal adhesion. `Maximum wipe tower print speed` keep 90 mm/s unless quality requires less. Purge-line spacing 120 %, extra flow for purging 100 %. Keep `No sparse layers (beta)` off.
- Tall towers (vendor guidance): wall type Rib, extra rib length and rib width 8 mm as documented examples, fillet wall on. A vendor screenshot for a 0.28 mm "Extra Draft" profile showed width 30 mm, prime volume 63 mm³, brim 5 mm; treat as a conservative example, not a default.
- Sequence and rollback as in the architecture reference. Sources: the vendor wiki's prime-tower troubleshooting page and FAQ, the OrcaSlicer wiki's prime tower page, and the vendor forum research thread (check current URLs).

## 3. Pressure advance on the U1

- Manual PA pattern gives a K value for a durable slicer filament profile. The printer's **Dynamic Flow Calibration** is an automatic pressure-lag calibration whose result the vendor documents as stored in firmware for the currently loaded filament, reset to the default K after unloading and not exposed back to the slicer (the slicer can only show pre-tested defaults). They are not interchangeable persistence mechanisms.
- Manual baseline for the direct-drive extruder: type DDE; general starting point 100 mm/s and 5000 mm/s²; faster PLA validation 150 mm/s and 8000 mm/s² only if max volumetric speed supports it. Do not calibrate at 200–300 mm/s merely because the machine can. See `fdm-material-and-diagnostics.md` for the sequence, empty-field behaviour and pitfalls.

## 4. Enclosure and top covers

The base machine is not a fully enclosed high-temperature chamber. Community top-cover modifications exist (a vendor forum collection thread, models on the common sharing sites, a cheap storage-box-based cover). Some include filter and fan wiring; forum notes mention an auxiliary-port fan connector rated about 24 V / 0.9 A with activation confusion, so verify current firmware and documentation before advising electrical modifications. A cover helps drafts, dust and filtering; it does not make the machine actively heated.

Auditing a downloaded cover or any multi-3MF package: see the package-audit method in the geometry skill's painted-3MF reference if installed, or `slicer-semantics-and-evidence.md` section 5. For a large community package, first establish which files form the standard build versus alternatives (an older body variant is not a mandatory companion of a newer one, and a large add-ons file with many plates must never be printed wholesale), open each as a full project, preserve supplied placement for parts near the 270 mm bed limit (no auto-arrange), swap the author's filament profiles for the user's calibrated ones, keep the author's anti-warp intent (for example disabled cooling for early layers on tall frame pieces), treat floating-region warnings as possibly documented connector-slot geometry and inspect Preview instead of enabling global supports, and print the shortest plate first as a process and adhesion check before committing to multi-hour plates. Recommend a genuinely full spool for the main colour. When the description says dimensions are in model pictures, retrieve the source images through the platform's public interface or the page itself, make a numbered contact sheet, read the full-resolution diagram, and record the upstream model ID so later revisions can be rechecked. Acrylic or other cut panels: order at nominal finished dimensions from a supplier that confirms actual thickness, protective film, a drawing upload and edge finish; describe contours by vertex coordinates rather than a generic web configurator's trapezoid fields, whose semantics differ between vendors. Do not recommend glass for a magnetic window panel. Dry-fit every magnet and verify its polarity before glue; use minimal adhesive only where retention needs it, and let any adhesive cure fully before installation.

Manual cutting is a conditional alternative only for plain, thin, unpatterned acrylic and straight cuts; for curves, thick, patterned or uncertain stock, or when unsure, use the supplier-cut route and provide the supplier a dimensioned drawing of the intended contour, specifying the nominal finished sizes and vertex coordinates (this reference ships no project-specific drawing). Follow the exact material and tool manufacturer's instructions for the scoring faces, depth and pass count, and wear eye protection. Leave the film or masking on, clamp a rigid straightedge, score repeatedly with a dedicated scoring tool under control, snap over a straight supported edge, and deburr lightly so the edges stay square and the nominal dimensions are kept. The scoring count and both-face scoring of the original case (3 mm extruded PMMA, about 15–20 passes on each face) are case-specific and not a rule for every sheet. Provenance: the acrylic maker's fabrication manual (Altuglas/Arkema, 2006), page 10, via a distributor copy: https://www.eplastics.com/pdf/acrylic-fabrication-manual.pdf.
